01 / OVERVIEW

Turning complex data into decisions.

The reporting environment involved multiple data sources and business requirements that needed to be translated into reliable Power BI reporting.

My work involved developing and maintaining reports, creating DAX measures, transforming data using Power Query, troubleshooting refresh and semantic-model issues, and improving report performance.

A major part of the work was understanding how the underlying business process should be represented in the data model before implementing the reporting logic.

02 / THE CHALLENGE

The difficult part wasn't the visual.

Building a Power BI report is more than placing charts on a canvas. The underlying data model, relationships, grain, keys, filter direction and business rules determine whether the final numbers can actually be trusted.

Several reporting requirements required historical business logic to be represented correctly — especially when tickets changed ownership or were rerouted between teams.

THE QUESTION What should the report show for a particular day when the underlying ticket data continues to change?

04 / MY APPROACH
01

Understand the grain

Before writing DAX, I first identify what one row represents, what uniquely identifies it, and how the business process produces those records.

02

Validate relationships

Relationships between tables, filter direction and key uniqueness need to be understood before relying on measures for reporting.

03

Translate business rules

Business requirements are converted into explicit reporting logic rather than relying only on the current state of the source data.

04

Validate the result

Results are compared against source-level records and known business scenarios to identify differences before finalizing the report.

05 / TECHNICAL CHALLENGE

Historical ticket ownership.

One of the challenging reporting scenarios involved tickets that could move between ITSM groups.

The report needed to represent the ticket according to the business ownership for the relevant day rather than simply using the latest ownership available in the current dataset.

08 / TAKEAWAY

Good BI starts before the chart.

The biggest takeaway from this work was that reliable reporting depends on understanding the business process and data model first. DAX and Power BI become much more effective when the underlying grain, relationships, historical behavior and business definitions are clear.
